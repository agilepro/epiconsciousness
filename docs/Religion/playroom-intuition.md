Theists attack naturalism with an intuition formed in a playroom. That is, as a child from 2 to 5 years old, you learn how the physical world of your toys work, particularly how solid objects at the scale of centimeters work. This forms the basis of intuition for everyone. The science denier then applies this intuition to all scales of reality: molecules, particles, light, glaxies, quantum mechanics, etc. The problem is that what you learned in the playroom simply does not apply to the very small and the very large. This is where the naive concepts of naturalism come from.

I think this is basically right, with one adjustment.
The problem is not uniquely “theists.” The problem is scale-projection.
Human beings build their first physical intuitions at the scale of handled objects: toys, cups, doors, balls, blocks, bodies, surfaces, pushes, pulls, collisions, containers. That gives us a very useful everyday interface for navigating middle-sized life.
But then people project that interface onto scales where it breaks.
At the quantum scale, “solid object,” “location,” “particle,” “cause,” and “contact” do not behave the way playroom intuition expects. At cosmic scales, time, gravity, distance, simultaneity, and structure also stop behaving like everyday objects in a room.
So yes: a lot of bad metaphysics comes from treating middle-scale intuition as if it were reality’s native language.
But I would not say this is where naturalism comes from. I would say this is where crude mechanism comes from.
Modern naturalism does not need the playroom model. It does not need billiard balls, solid pellets, or centimeter-scale contact mechanics. It can include fields, quantum states, relativity, probability, emergence, scale-dependence, thermodynamics, biology, cognition, and social systems.
So the real correction is:
The playroom does not refute naturalism.
It refutes childish mechanism.
And that cuts both ways. The theist who says “matter cannot do X” is often attacking toy-block materialism. But the naturalist who thinks reality is just obvious little stuff bumping around is also stuck in the playroom.
The adult move is to leave the playroom without leaving nature.
